## Four Businesses a Day

A restaurant is not one business a day. It is four. Breakfast. The slow period after it. Lunch. The afternoon. Each one has its own sales and its own crew.

Labor is the biggest cost I control, and I control it by the hour. Who comes in at seven. Who goes home at one thirty.

I can get sales by the hour in one report. I can get labor for the day in another. Nothing puts them side by side in the day parts I actually run. So a strong lunch pays for a dead afternoon, and the daily number is all mashed together.

## The Old Way

For years, you and I asked for that report the way we wanted it, from every vendor we had. We got a custom quote every time, or a promise for next year. Sometimes we got the report, and the package it came in failed everywhere else.

## What Changed

This week Square put up a page called "Run your whole business with the agent you already use." Read it as an operator and it says one thing. You do not have to wait.

Square is doing what everyone else in restaurant POS is scared to do. Not a new app. Not a new dashboard. Your own AI agent, with hands inside your Square account, and a place to host what it builds.

Two pieces make it work. The first is an AI subscription, the same kind you would use to write an email. Mine is Claude. The second is Square's tools: a command line called the Square CLI, and a set of skills that teach the AI how Square works. Orders. Timecards. Locations.

I say what I want. My AI builds the app with Square's tools. Square hosts it.

## One Sentence

Here is the sentence I said:

> Build me a day parts board. Breakfast is seven to ten thirty, mid-morning is ten thirty to eleven thirty, lunch is eleven thirty to one thirty, afternoon is one thirty to close. For each day part show me net sales, ticket count, average ticket, and labor hours with labor as a percent of sales, pulled from Square orders and timecards, today against the same weekday last week, one card per day part on one screen I can put on the office wall.

Then I watched it get built. The video shows the whole build in real time, and the board running on real numbers from my cafes. Dollar figures and location names are hidden. The percentages, tickets, and hours are real.

## The Take

The distance between knowing what your business needs and having the tool used to be a vendor. Now it is a sentence, in your voice, and your natural language. However you talk to your business, it is right.

And for the proverbial naysayers, I know dozens of sellers doing this the hard way already. There are no style points in business for doing it the hard way. This is where everyone is wrong. People are going to use AI, but they want to use their own.

I did not hire a developer. I did not buy another product. I described the thing in the words I use in the restaurant.

Describe the thing you have wanted for years. Then come back and tell me what you built.
